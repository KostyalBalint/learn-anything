Every request to the API server goes through three gates: **authentication**
(who are you), **authorization** (may you), **admission** (is this object
acceptable). RBAC is the second one, and it's built from four objects that split
along two axes: *what may be done* versus *who may do it*, and *one namespace*
versus *the whole cluster*.

A rule is a boring tuple — verbs on resources in API groups:

```yaml
rules:
  - apiGroups: [""]                 # "" is the core group
    resources: ["pods", "pods/log"]
    verbs: ["get", "list", "watch"]
```

Note `pods/log` — subresources are separate. Granting `get pods` does **not**
grant log access, and `create pods/exec` (shell into a Pod) is a separate,
much more dangerous permission than `create pods`.

## Additive only, no deny

RBAC has no deny rule. Permissions are the union of every binding that applies to
you, and nothing subtracts. So "everything except Secrets" is not expressible —
you enumerate what's allowed and Secrets simply aren't in the list. If someone
also has `view` bound cluster-wide, they can read Secrets and no policy you write
can take it back. You remove the binding instead.

The corollary: `kubectl auth can-i --list --as=system:serviceaccount:prod:api`
is the only reliable way to know what a subject can do. Reading the Role tells
you one piece of a union.

## The trap: ClusterRole + RoleBinding

The most-misread pair in Kubernetes:

| Binding | Role reference | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RoleBinding` | `Role` | Those rules, in the RoleBinding's namespace. |
| `RoleBinding` | `ClusterRole` | Those rules, **only in the RoleBinding's namespace**. |
| `ClusterRoleBinding` | `ClusterRole` | Those rules, in **every** namespace, plus cluster-scoped resources. |

Row two is the useful one, and the one people fear. Referencing the built-in
`edit` ClusterRole from a RoleBinding in `team-a` grants edit **only in
`team-a`** — the ClusterRole is a reusable rule set, and the *binding* decides the
scope. Row three is where the accident lives: the same reference in a
ClusterRoleBinding hands over the entire cluster.

Cluster-scoped resources (nodes, PVs, namespaces themselves) can only be granted
by a ClusterRoleBinding — there's no namespace to scope them to.

## The built-ins

`view` (read, no Secrets), `edit` (write, no RBAC), `admin` (edit + manage RBAC
within a namespace), `cluster-admin` (everything, everywhere). Bind the first
three with a **RoleBinding** per namespace and they're reasonable defaults. Bind
them with a ClusterRoleBinding and you've quietly gone cluster-wide.

Also worth knowing: `escalate` and `bind`. Ordinarily you cannot create a Role
with permissions you don't have — that's what stops `edit` from becoming
`cluster-admin`. Those two verbs remove that check, so granting them is granting
everything, one step removed.
